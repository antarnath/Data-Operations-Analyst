Root Cause Verdict

The pipeline is not losing encounters — it is rejecting them at the scrubber. 282 encounters recorded in the EHR never reached the billing database, yet the same patient + provider combination bills successfully for 6,330 other encounters. — of all encounters imported cleanly, and of the encounters that reached the billing DB, — matched the EHR exactly. The connectivity and the ETL join are both healthy: this is not a transport failure.

Inspecting the stranded encounters shows the failure is concentrated in the CPT scrubber. Internal operational markers are being written into the CPT code field — NORCM TOS115 SP$90 sp90 — none of which are valid AMA/CMS CPT codes. A record carrying any one of these fails CPT-format validation and the entire claim is rejected, taking the valid, billable E/M lines down with it.

Business impact: — of charge value is unbilled or miscoded — — of total charge value. Of that, — sits in claims that are already clean and fully billable and are being lost purely because of a non-billable string in one field. Those are recoverable within the standard timely-filing window.

Recommended Solution

Sequenced by time-to-recovery and implementation effort.

1

Recover stranded claims — 48 hours

Re-run the — Critical-tier encounters after stripping invalid codes. All billable content is already present in the EHR; these require no clinical re-work, only re-adjudication.

2

Scrub non-CPT values at source

Deploy a mapping layer that quarantines internal markers (NORCM, SP*, TOS*) instead of letting them fail validation and reject the whole claim. Prevents recurrence at the point of origin.

3

Row-level validation on CPT

Add a format check (^9\d{4}$) plus a valid-code lookup in the ingestion layer. Flag, don't reject — a single bad line must never take a valid claim down with it.

4

Daily reconciliation control

Automate this comparison as a standing control. Alert when EHR encounter count and DB encounter count diverge beyond threshold, so leakage is caught in hours rather than at month-end close.

5

Investigate over-billing & ghost claims

— was billed beyond EHR support plus — across — encounters billed with no source-of-truth record. These carry denial and clawback exposure and warrant priority review ahead of recovery work.

Expected outcome: closing the Critical tier and the scrubber defect recovers the majority of at-risk value inside one filing cycle, and the daily control prevents recurrence by detecting divergence within 24 hours.
